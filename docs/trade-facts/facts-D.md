## Licensing and restricted building work

**https://www.lbp.govt.nz/about-us/about-the-lbp-scheme/overview/** (carpenters, concreters)
- RBW since 1 March 2012, carried out or supervised by LBPs: "The category of restricted building work (RBW) came into effect on 1 March 2012. RBW involves critical areas of residential home design and construction that can only be carried out or supervised by LBPs (*and certain registered professionals)."
- Seven licence classes incl Carpentry and Foundations: "The LBP scheme has 7 different licence classes" ... "Carpentry" ... "Foundations."
- Foundations are primary structure: "the primary structure of a building. This work contributes to the resistance of loads that are vertical (such as walls and columns) and horizontal (such as foundation, floors and roofs)"
- Licence assesses skills, licenses RBW (LBP is not insurance FAQ): "to assess and record building practitioners as having certain skills and knowledge relevant to building work; and to license building practitioners so that they can carry out or supervise restricted building work."
- Board complaints: "Complaints may be about doing work negligently or incompetently; doing or supervising RBW when not appropriately licensed; or not providing the necessary records of building work for RBW."

**https://www.lbp.govt.nz/for-lbps/restricted-building-work/** (carpenters callout, concreters)

- Definition: "the building is a residential house of any height, or a small to medium apartment building of less than 10 metres in total height" ... "the work relates to the primary structure, the external moisture management in any building, and/or the design of fire safety systems in a multi-unit building."

## Building Act consumer protection (MBIE)

**https://www.building.govt.nz/projects-and-consents/why-contracts-are-valuable/implied-warranties-and-defects** (carpenters, concreters, handymen)
- 12 months: "If any defects in the building work emerge within 12 months of the completed build date, your builder has an obligation to fix them."
- 10 years, written or verbal, any cost: "All residential building work is covered by implied warranties. They apply: for up to 10 years regardless of whether you have a written contract or what the contract terms are; regardless of the cost of your building project."
- "These warranties apply automatically to all contracts for building work on a residential house, whether written or verbal."

**https://www.building.govt.nz/projects-and-consents/why-contracts-are-valuable/consumer-protection-disclosure-and-checklist** (carpenters, concreters FAQ, handymen)
- On request, any size: "Your builder must provide this information if you ask for it, regardless of the size of the work."
- $30,000: "If your building work is going to cost $30,000 or more (including GST) they must provide it anyway."
- Insurance content: "information about insurance policies the contractor has, or intends to have, in relation to the building work – they must specify the amount of the cover and any relevant exclusions"

**https://www.building.govt.nz/projects-and-consents/why-contracts-are-valuable/contracts-for-your-building-project** (supporting: disclosure applies to each person the homeowner contracts with)
- "Each person you have a contract with must give you a consumer protection standard checklist and disclosure statement if the work meets the $30,000 threshold or you ask for this information."

## Schedule 1 exempt building work

**https://www.building.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/projects-and-consents/building-work-consent-not-required-guidance.pdf** (MBIE, "Sixth edition – Version 2 – January 2026"; handymen, landscapers, fencers)
- Exempt work must comply with Code and other laws: "All building work, whether it needs a building consent or is exempt, must still comply with the Building Code and other relevant legislation including:" ... "Resource Management Act 1991"
- Owner responsible: "As a home or building owner, you are responsible for: determining whether your building work is exempt from requiring a building consent or not"
- Insurance effect: "Non-compliant building work may affect any insurance cover on the building, as well as any future sale and purchase of the property."
- Certificate of acceptance: "If you carry out unconsented building work which was not exempt at the time the work was undertaken, you will need to apply to your local council for a certificate of acceptance."
- Exemption 1: "The repair and maintenance of a building product or an assembly incorporated in or associated with a building, provided that a comparable building product or assembly is used." / "Replacement of a building product or an assembly ... (b) the replacement is in the same position." / exclusions "(b) complete or substantial replacement of a building product or an assembly contributing to the building's structural behaviour or fire-safety properties" and "(d) sanitary plumbing or drainlaying under the Plumbers, Gasfitters, and Drainlayers Act 2006."
- Exemption 24 (decks): "Building work in connection with a deck, platform, bridge, boardwalk, or the like from which it is not possible to fall more than 1.5 metres even if it collapses."
- Exemption 21 (fences): "Building work in connection with a fence or hoarding in each case not exceeding 2.5 metres in height above the supporting ground." / "Subclause (1) does not include a fence or hoarding to restrict access to a residential pool." / "Any building work relating to fences (which includes garden walls) and hoardings not exceeding 2.5 metres high is covered by this exemption" / measuring: "measure the vertical distance between the top of the structure and the supporting ground directly below." / Fencing Act and resource consent: "Note that you will still need to comply with the requirements of the Fencing Act 1978 for boundary fences. In many cases, district plans made under the Resource Management Act 1991 may also require you to obtain a resource consent for fences over a certain height (usually over two metres)." / pool fence: "A building consent is required, and the fence installed before the pool is filled with water."
- Exemption 20 (retaining walls): "Building work in connection with a retaining wall that— (a) retains not more than 1.5 metres depth of ground; and (b) does not support any surcharge or any load additional to the load of that ground (for example, the load of vehicles)." / surcharge list: "Vehicle driveways / Parking spaces / Swimming pools / Buildings / Other retaining walls / Sloping ground above the top of the retaining wall" / partial height: "A retaining wall ranges in height from 900 millimetres to 1.8 metres. The part of the retaining wall that exceeds the maximum allowable height of 1.5 metres will require a building consent." / barrier: "If there is a fall of one metre or more from the retaining wall, you may be required to install a safety barrier (under Building Code clause F4 – Safety from falling)."
- Exemption 41: "This exemption covers building work relating to some retaining walls in rural zones as long as the design is carried out or reviewed by a Chartered Professional Engineer." (section title "13.3. Retaining walls not exceeding three metres depth of ground (exemption 41)")

## WorkSafe

**https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/dust/silica-dust-in-the-workplace/** (concreters)
- "Silica is a natural substance found in concrete, bricks, rocks, stone ... sand and clay. RCS dust is created when materials containing silica are cut, ground, drilled, sanded, polished or otherwise disturbed."
- "concrete drilling, cutting, grinding, fettling, mixing, handling, dry shoveling, tunneling."
- Diseases: "Silicosis: ... The effects of silicosis are permanent." / "Lung cancer: may occur..." / "Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)"
- Duty: "As a PCBU, you have a duty to eliminate, or use controls to minimise worker exposure to the hazard of, and risks from, RCS dust." / "You must eliminate risks that arise from your work so far as is reasonably practicable."
- Controls: "Use on-tool water suppression systems to keep dust out of the air" / "tools used for cutting, grinding or polishing concrete and masonry should provide water to the blade and/or be fitted with an on-tool extraction system." / Don't: "Dry sweep work areas" / "Use compressed air to blow away dust"
- Health monitoring: "Provide health monitoring for all your workers who may be exposed to RCS dust."

**https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/dust/wood-dust-controlling-the-risks/** (carpenters)
- "Inhaling dust into the lungs can cause breathing problems and lead to lung diseases such as occupational asthma and lung cancer."
- "The presence of glues, resins, formaldehyde and other wood treatment chemicals in some wood products increase the health risks from wood dust."
- "WorkSafe expects so far as is reasonably practicable, preference to be given to controls that protect multiple at-risk workers at a time. For example local exhaust ventilation (LEV) will protect everyone in the workplace but respiratory protective equipment (RPE) only protects the person wearing it." / "Use on-tool extraction on saws and grinders to control wood dust at source."
- "Where workers are routinely exposed to wood dust you should arrange health monitoring for them."

**https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/working-at-height/working-at-height-in-nz/** (carpenters, handymen)
- "In New Zealand, there is no minimum height threshold for working at height – any fall risk from any height must be managed."
- "This includes working above or below ground level, using ladders, scaffolds, working on roofs, or operating near unprotected edges."

**https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/excavation/excavation-safety-gpg/** (concreters, landscapers, fencers)
- "Always assume there are underground services at the site. Make sure the services are located and confirmed by the service owner. Some service plans are not accurate and the actual position and depth may be quite different."
- "service plans from each service owner" / "Use plans, mark-outs, locators and safe digging practices when working near buried services." / "use detectors to locate services laterally as excavation depth progresses"
- Notification: "Employers and the person in control of the workplace must notify WorkSafe at least 24 hours' before doing any hazardous work" ... "any commercial logging or tree-felling" ... "people have to work in an excavation that is more than 1.5 m deep and which is deeper than it is wide at the top"
- Shoring: "every employer must, so far as is reasonably practicable, ensure that, where any face of any excavation is more than 1.5 m high, that face is shored."

**https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/about-us/news-and-media/risk-involved-when-working-near-buried-power-cables/** (concreters, landscapers, fencers)

## How liability cover works (insurer's published wording)

**https://www.vero.co.nz/documents/business/vero-combined-broadform-liability-07-24.pdf** (all five)
- Insuring clause: "We will indemnify you for all amounts you become legally liable to pay as direct compensation consequent upon: (a) personal injury; or (b) damage to property; happening within the geographical limits during the period of insurance as a result of an occurrence in connection with the business."
- Products aggregate: "(b) any one period of insurance for all claims in respect of products"; definition "Products anything ... supplied, sold, distributed, grown, extracted, manufactured, constructed, erected, installed, serviced, repaired or treated by any of the persons insured in the course of the business after it has ceased to be in the possession of or under the control of any of the persons insured"
- Defective work exclusion: "4. Defective work Where your liability is for the cost of performing, completing, correcting or improving any work done or undertaken by any of the persons insured. This exclusion will not apply to legal liability for resultant damage to other separate property or parts that any of the persons insured have not been working on."
- Fines: "6. Fines and penalties In respect of any fines, penalties, reparation, exemplary, aggravated or liquidated damages."
- Pollutants: "11. Pollutants Directly or indirectly caused by or arising out of pollutants unless caused by or arising out of an identifiable and sudden accidental and unexpected release ... of pollutants that takes place in its entirety at a specific time and place." Definition: "any solid, liquid, gaseous or thermal irritant or contaminant including ... chemicals"
- Product repair: "12. Product repair or replacement In respect of any amount payable for the cost of recalling, withdrawing, replacing or repairing products"
- Care, custody or control: "14. Property in care, custody or control Owned, occupied or held in trust by or in the care, custody or control of any of the persons insured other than: (a) premises that are not owned or rented by any of the persons insured but at which any of the persons insured is undertaking work in connection with the business"
- Underground services: "15. Underground services Consisting of underground services, pipes or cables or legal liability arising out of that damage regardless of how the damage is caused unless the person insured has taken all reasonable steps immediately prior to the commencement of the work to establish the position of such services and has taken all reasonable steps to avoid damage in which case a $5,000 excess for Underground Services will apply to all such valid claims."
- Vibration: "17. Vibration or weakening of support Directly or indirectly caused by vibration or by the withdrawal or weakening of support of any structure or land." Extension 18: "we will cover you for all amounts that you become legally liable to pay as direct compensation as a result of personal injury or damage to property caused by vibration or the withdrawal or weakening of support of any structure or land. Our liability under this extension will not exceed $500,000 ... An excess of $5,000 will apply" (used only as "its own limit and excess"; no dollar figures on the pages)
- Defective workmanship extension: "We will cover you for your legal liability to pay direct compensation ... consequent upon accidental damage to property on which you are or have been working, where the damage is caused by your defective workmanship" / "Our liability under this extension will not exceed $100,000 unless an alternative limit is shown in the schedule" (used only as "its own limit")
- Duty of care condition: "The persons insured will take reasonable precautions to prevent personal injury and damage to property and to: (a) comply with all obligations and regulations set out in any legislation applicable or imposed by any authority"
- Statutory liability: "We will pay on your behalf any fine under an Act and defence costs arising out of a claim made against you" (read with WorkSafe s29 statement above: HSWA fines cannot be insured, so pages only say statutory liability can meet defence costs)
